HALO: Hazard-Aware Landing Optimization

Choosing where to land — and how to get there — at the same time.

Overview

Landing on rough terrain — a boulder field on the Moon, a crater rim on Mars — requires answering two questions at once: where is it safe to land, and can we actually fly there from the current state? Most systems answer these separately, which can select a landing site the vehicle cannot reach.

HALO (Hazard-Aware Landing Optimization) couples the two decisions. It evaluates candidate landing sites using terrain data from onboard perception while simultaneously checking the feasibility and cost of the divert trajectory to each one, producing a safe site and the fuel-efficient trajectory to reach it.

This line of work supports precision landing for lunar and planetary missions, where late hazard detection leaves little time and fuel to react.
