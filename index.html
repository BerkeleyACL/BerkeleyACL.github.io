About the lab

The Autonomous Controls Laboratory (ACL), led by Prof. Behçet Açıkmeşe, has relocated to the University of California, Berkeley after a decade at the University of Washington. Our work bridges control theory, optimization, and aerospace engineering: we design algorithms with provable guarantees that run in real time on flight hardware.

A hallmark of the lab is taking theory all the way to practice. Methods developed by the group — including lossless convexification and successive convexification for onboard trajectory optimization — have informed planetary landing guidance and reusable rocket flight, and our open-source solvers and tools are used by researchers and practitioners worldwide.

Research themes

Five threads run through the lab's work. Each combines rigorous theory with algorithms designed for real-time, onboard implementation.

Trajectory Optimization

Real-time, onboard trajectory generation for landing, rendezvous, and proximity operations with hard state and control constraints.

First-Order Optimization Algorithms

Custom solvers — such as QOCO, our quadratic-objective conic optimizer — built for speed, reliability, and embedded deployment.

Robust Control

Feedback synthesis that guarantees performance and constraint satisfaction despite uncertainty, disturbances, and model error.

Optimal Recursive Decision-Making

Sequential decision-making under uncertainty, with convex formulations that make optimal policies computationally tractable.

Markov Decision Processes

Density control and policy synthesis for large-scale stochastic systems, including swarm guidance and probabilistic safety.
